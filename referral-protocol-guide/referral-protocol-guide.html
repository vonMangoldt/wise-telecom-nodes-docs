    Referral Protocol Guide

    Earn on the deposits you bring to the Wise Telecom Nodes vaults.

    The Wise Telecom Nodes vaults pay their depositors a fixed interest rate. The referral system adds a second income on top: when you bring a depositor to a vault, you earn a commission on that depositor's principal for as long as it stays, paid in USDC on Ethereum mainnet and priced week by week against a published rate table. Nothing is taken from the depositor; the commission is paid by the protocol.

    This guide is cut by the question you bring to it. Depositors, referrers, integrators, front end developers, protocol developers and reviewers each have their own chapter, and a thing is explained in one place and linked from the others.

    What the Referral System Is

    The system adds three parts to the running vault diamonds.

    A registry per chain. The registry records which referrer a depositor named, once, and keeps every chain in agreement about it. Every vault on a chain reads and writes the same registry, the registries exchange their records over Chainlink CCIP, and the earliest assignment wins everywhere. A consent gated correction lane lets the operator propose a different referrer, which the user can veto for three days.

    Cohort facets on every vault. New facets on each vault diamond record, week by week, how much referred stake and how much own stake each referrer had present in that vault, in token seconds and by age. They ship those weekly records to the hub as flush envelopes, over CCIP from Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood and by direct call on mainnet. Nothing already deployed is edited; the facets are added through the vaults' three day timelocks.

    A hub on Ethereum mainnet. The hub receives the weekly records from every vault on every chain, sums them per referrer and week, prices each closed week against the rate table in force for that week and credits a claimable bucket per source chain and referrer. It is its own pot, funded by plain USDC transfers from the protocol, and pays claims from it.

    Every number in this guide is read from the code. Where a figure is a genesis value that we can later change through a timelocked lever, the guide says so.

    Weeks and Years

    A week is the Unix timestamp divided by one week, so every week starts at the same instant on every chain, and a week is closed once that instant has passed. Every quantity in the system is counted in whole weeks: the records a vault writes, the cursors a flush advances, the envelopes the hub receives, the rate tables and the elevation windows.

    A year is 52 weeks, 364 days. Referred credit ages in a ring of 52 weekly slots from year one into year two and then into mature credit, and every rate in the rate table is a rate per 52 week year. Read against a calendar year of 365.25 days a stated rate pays about 0.34 percent more; the referrer chapter explains why in The 52 Week Year.

    A source is one vault on one chain as the hub sees it. The hub keys everything it receives by source, because the same vault product has the same address on every chain it runs on.

    Several Vault Systems Under One Referral Umbrella

    The referral system is one umbrella over several vault systems. The nine World Mobile vaults are system 0. The Sovren vault, product wsnusdc on Ethereum mainnet, a USDC vault whose deposits finance Sovren infrastructure nodes, is system 1. Later node systems built on the same code can join as further systems. Every system runs under the same settings: one rate table, one cap, one ladder, one courier share and one payout pot at the hub.

    A referrer's ladder inputs sum over every vault of every system, so 500,000 referred in a World Mobile vault plus 500,000 in the Sovren vault counts as 1,000,000 on the ladder. The hub tags every source vault with its system number for reporting only; pricing never reads the tag. Money never moves between systems: no move peer and no bridge peer is ever registered between a Sovren vault and a World Mobile vault, on any chain.

    The Sovren vault joins the referral system alongside the World Mobile rollout, through its own upgrade rounds and the registry's allow lane. Its referral facets are the same code under the Sovren contract names, built and rehearsed in its own repository, so a referrer sees one behaviour in every system; the fourteen facets of its registry round are routed since 2026-10-28 and its flush round since 2026-11-02, listed in Addresses; its registry pointer is wired, its recorder armed and the hub accepts it as a reporter since 2026-11-02, so it measures from that day. Its record is allowlisted on the mainnet registry since 2026-10-25, its flush hub pointer is wired since 2026-11-05 and its books ship to the hub since that day, so the join is complete. Adding a system is an operator procedure, a configuration record, the registry allow lane and the hub source tag, never a redeploy of anything shared.

    The Sovren Vault

    The Sovren vault is Wise Sovren Nodes, a USDC vault on Ethereum mainnet whose deposits finance Sovren infrastructure nodes. Its share token is wsnUSDC, its diamond address is listed in Addresses, it runs on mainnet only, and it is built on the same vault code as the World Mobile vaults with its own exit queue incentive ladder. Its source is public at wise-sovren-nodes-v1.

    For a depositor nothing differs. The referral deposit entrypoints, the write once referrer, the three day correction veto and the queue rules of For Depositors are the same functions on the Sovren diamond, and the referrer named there is recorded in the mainnet registry that the three World Mobile mainnet vaults share. For a referrer the Sovren vault is one more source: its weekly records reach the hub by direct call in the flush transaction itself, because the vault and the hub share a chain, and the commission lands in the mainnet bucket beside the World Mobile mainnet commission. The ladder sums Sovren stake with every other stake, as the section above describes, and the Sovren queue treats a join exactly as a World Mobile queue does. Shares never move or bridge between the Sovren vault and a World Mobile vault.

    The join runs in the same order as a World Mobile vault's rollout. The mainnet registry admits the vault through its three day allow lane, the hub tags its source as system 1, the Sovren diamond routes the registry round and then the flush round through its own three day selector timelock, and the hub grants it the reporter role through the lever timelock. The referral facets are the same code under the Sovren contract names, built and rehearsed in the Sovren repository; the two queue facets additionally carry the Sovren incentive ladder and let a standing order leave a lane that was closed to new joins. Their addresses are added to Addresses as each round lands.

    For Depositors

    Name your referrer once, on any chain, and nothing else changes for you.

    When you deposit into a Wise Telecom Nodes vault you can name a referrer: the person or integrator who brought you. Your referrer then earns a commission on your principal for as long as it stays in the vaults. The commission is paid by the protocol, not by you: your deposit, your interest and your exits work exactly as they would without a referrer.

    This chapter covers what you do and what it means for you. What your referrer earns, and how, is in For Referrers.

    How it Works:

    Naming a Referrer:

    You name a referrer by depositing through one of the referral deposit entrypoints of a vault: depositWithReferral, depositAndClaimInterestWithReferral, depositAndCompoundInterestWithReferral, or their Permit2 twins. Connect your wallet, open the deposit screen, enter the referrer address (or follow a referral link that fills it in) and confirm. The assignment runs first and the deposit follows as a plain deposit.

    The assignment is written once. The first referrer you name is recorded in the chain's referral registry and shared by every vault on that chain. A later deposit that names a different referrer is accepted as a deposit and the referrer argument is ignored. A zero referrer or your own address reverts. A vault, the registry or the hub can never be a referrer, and a referrer whose own referral chain leads back to you is refused, so two accounts cannot refer each other.

    The Referral Link:

    A referral link carries the referrer's address. The front end keeps that address and writes it into the referrer field of the deposit call it sends; that exact address is what the registry records for you. If you already have a referrer, a good front end shows your recorded referrer instead of the link's address, because the link can no longer change it.

    One Referrer Across All Chains:

    Your referrer is one fact across the whole mesh, not one per chain. Each registry stamps an assignment with a key made of the time of the assignment and the rank of the chain it was made on, and the registries exchange these keys over CCIP. When two registries disagree about you, the lower key wins, which means the earlier assignment wins; the chain ranks only break a tie between two assignments made in the same second, in a fixed order.

    Suppose you name referrer A on Base on Monday and, before the two registries have talked, name referrer B on Arbitrum on Tuesday. Each chain records what it saw. As soon as the Base pair reaches Arbitrum, Arbitrum's registry sees the earlier key, switches you to A and marks the stake you already hold there for relocation from B's book to A's book. The Arbitrum pair can never win on Base, because its key is later. From then on every chain answers A, and A earns on your stake everywhere. Weeks that were already priced under B stay with B, because a closed week keeps its verdict.

    The exchange is permissionless: anyone may pay the small cross chain fee to broadcast your pair to the other chains with broadcastSync, our courier does so for every new assignment, and a bridge of your own shares carries the pair along as well. Once you have been assigned and synced, a later deposit that names another referrer on any chain is accepted as a deposit and the referrer argument is ignored, exactly as on a single chain.

    Can My Referrer Be Changed?

    Only with your consent. We (the protocol operator, called master in the code) may propose a different referrer for you with proposeCorrectReferrer. The proposal opens a three day window. During that window you can cancel it unilaterally with cancelCorrectReferrer, and so can we. Only after the three days may we execute it with executeCorrectReferrer. A proposal for a user who already has a referrer must be made on the chain where your current key was minted, which is the chain of your earliest referral deposit or of the last correction, so you veto on a chain where you have already acted.

    A correction takes effect for the future only. Everything already priced at the hub stays with the old referrer. Your recorded stake moves to the new referrer's book through the same relocation path a cross chain sync flip uses, and the corrected pair carries a key that beats every copy of the old pair, so one broadcast carries it to every chain.

    The same lane lets us attach a referrer to a user who never named one. We propose on any chain of the mesh, you have the same three days to cancel, and the execute records the pair with the same winning key. No book moves, because there was no referrer before. A referral deposit you make during the window kills the proposal, because the proposal is bound to the exact key you held when it was made.

    A front end that watches ReferralCorrectionProposed on your chain's registry can show you a cancel button for the whole three days.

    What Changes for You?

    Does a Referral Cost Me Anything?

    No. Your deposit, your interest rate, your claims and your exits are the plain vault flows; the referral assignment runs first and then the deposit continues as if you had used the plain entrypoint. The commission your referrer earns is paid by the protocol from its own pot on mainnet. Nothing is deducted from your principal or your interest, and a referrer can never touch your funds.

    Does My Own Stake Matter?

    Only if you refer others. Your own stake never earns commission for anyone, but if you become a referrer yourself, your own stake raises your rate on the ladder described in What a Referrer Earns.

    What Do Moves and Bridges Do?

    A move between two vaults of the same product on the same chain and a bridge to your own address on another chain keep your referrer's credit, with exactly the age it had. A transfer to another address, a withdrawal through the queue and a burn end the credit for the amount that left; a new deposit builds it again. None of this changes your own balance or interest.

    What if I Join the Exit Queue?

    Joining the queue is a withdrawal for commission purposes. A vault has no direct withdrawal; you exit by joining the queue with joinQue, which moves the queued shares from you into the vault's escrow by an internal transfer. That transfer runs the same hook as any transfer, so at the moment of the join your referrer's book is reduced by the queued amount exactly as a sale would reduce it, newest credit first, and the presence counted for your referrer stops for that amount from that instant.

    Leaving the queue unfilled with leaveQue or reduceQueAmount returns the shares to you as plain balance and restores nothing for your referrer. The buyer who takes your queued shares is credited to no referrer. Your own position is unaffected: the shares come back in full if you leave, and you are paid in full if the order fills.

    Conclusion

    You name a referrer once, on any chain, and the earliest assignment wins everywhere. Nothing is taken from you, your referrer earns from the protocol on the principal you keep in the vaults, and a change of referrer needs your consent. The only thing to remember is that joining the exit queue ends your referrer's credit for the queued amount, which is exactly what a withdrawal would do.

    For Referrers

    What you earn, how it is counted and how you collect it.

    As a referrer you earn a commission on the principal of the depositors who named you, for as long as it stays in the vaults. The commission is a yearly rate on the referred stake, counted week by week, priced at the hub on Ethereum mainnet and paid to you in USDC. Your own stake never earns commission, but it raises your rate.

    How a depositor names you, and how the earliest assignment wins across chains, is in For Depositors. This chapter covers the money: the tiers and rates, elevated status, how weeks are priced, how your weeks reach the hub, how you claim and what to expect.

    How it Works:

    What a Referrer Earns:

    You earn commission on the referred principal present in the vaults, that is on the stake of the depositors who named you. Your own stake never earns commission; it only raises the rate. The commission is a yearly rate applied to the referred stake for the time it was present, week by week.

    Every week has its own rate for every referrer, computed from two inputs the hub measures for that week alone: the average referred stake and the average own stake, each summed over every vault of every chain. Each input is placed on the ladder below and gives a tier. The year one rate is the sum of the two tiers, capped at the cap.

    Average stake in USD (six decimal units) Tier
    5,000 or more 1 percent
    10,000 or more 2 percent
    20,000 or more 3 percent
    30,000 or more 4 percent
    50,000 or more 5 percent
    100,000 or more 6 percent
    500,000 or more 7 percent
    1,000,000 or more 8 percent

    The genesis cap is 10 percent (1,000 basis points). Every rate is a nominal rate per 52 week year; read against a calendar year the 10 percent cap is about 10.03 percent, and the commission is paid out in USDC rather than compounded.

    • Worked example one: you hold 50,000 of your own stake and your referees hold 100,000 in a week. Your tiers are 5 and 6, so your year one rate is capped to 10 percent on year one credit for that week. On 100,000 of year one credit the week pays 100,000 times 10 percent times 7 over 364, about 192 USDC.
    • Worked example two: you hold no own stake and your referees hold 20,000. Your rate is 3 percent on year one credit, about 11.5 USDC for the week.
    • Worked example three: you hold 5,000 of your own stake and your referees hold 4,000. Your own stake reaches the 1 percent tier but 4,000 of referred stake is below the first threshold, so the week pays 1 percent on the 4,000, about 0.77 USDC.

    Referred credit ages. A deposit is year one credit from the week it was made until the 52nd week boundary after the start of its credit week, year two credit for the 52 weeks after that, and mature credit from then on. Year one credit pays the ladder rate described above. Year two credit pays 5 percent (500 basis points) and mature credit pays 1 percent (100 basis points), but only in weeks where your uncapped ladder sum reaches the cap. A referrer below the cap earns nothing on year two and mature credit that week.

    The ladder, the cap and the two tail rates are one rate table. We can publish a new table with pushRateTable, but only for a future week, and every past week keeps the table that was in force when it closed. The first table is the genesis table above.

    Several Vault Systems Under One Referral Umbrella:

    Your ladder does not care which vault system a stake sits in. The nine World Mobile vaults are system 0 and the Sovren vault (wsnusdc on mainnet) is system 1, and both run under the same rate table, cap, ladder, courier share and payout pot. Your referred input and your own input each sum over every vault of every system, so 500,000 referred in a World Mobile vault plus 500,000 in the Sovren vault counts as 1,000,000 and reaches the top rung. Commission still accrues per source chain, so Sovren commission lands in your mainnet bucket beside the World Mobile mainnet commission.

    Money never moves between systems: a referee cannot move or bridge shares from a World Mobile vault into the Sovren vault or back, so credit never relocates across systems either. The Sovren vault is a full member of the referral system since 2026-11-05; the overview explains the umbrella in Several Vault Systems Under One Referral Umbrella.

    Elevated Referrers:

    We can elevate a referrer. An elevated referrer is priced as at the cap for every week inside its elevation window whatever its inputs are: 10 percent on year one credit, 5 percent on year two credit and 1 percent on mature credit under the genesis table. A grant opens at a future week and waits the same three day timelock as every other grant on the hub. An end closes the window at a future week and takes effect at once. A closed week keeps its verdict, so an elevation can never lower what you were already paid, and a week inside an ended window stays elevated for good even when it is delivered to the hub later. A referrer's history holds at most eight windows.

    If you are an integrator or partner who is to be elevated, the one rule that matters is the address you use as referrer; it is explained in The Elevated Referrer Address Rule.

    The 52 Week Year:

    Every quantity in the system is counted in whole weeks: the crumbs a vault writes, the cursors a flush advances, the envelopes the hub receives, the rate tables and the elevation windows are all keyed by the week number, which is the Unix timestamp divided by one week. The ring that ages a referee's credit from year one into year two and then into mature credit has 52 slots, one per week, so a cohort's year one is exactly 52 weeks long and ends on a week boundary. A calendar year would end in the middle of a week, and that week would need two rates at once.

    The year the rates refer to is therefore the 52 week year of 364 days, and every rate in the rate table is a rate per 52 week year. The hub pays each week as the stake present that week multiplied by the week's rate and by seven days over 364 days: a referrer on the 10 percent tier earns 10 percent of its weekly averages summed over 52 weeks. Read against a calendar year of 365.25 days this is about 0.34 percent more than the stated figure (365.25 divided by 364), and that is the whole effect. The ring and the pricing use the same year, so no week is ever paid twice or skipped.

    A full week of referred stake pays one fifty second of the yearly rate on it, floored once per week.

    The commission of one vault, one referrer and one week is the referred token seconds of each lane multiplied by that lane's rate for the week and divided by the 364 day year and by 10,000 basis points, floored once per week.

    How Weeks Are Priced:

    The vaults measure and the hub prices. For every referrer and every closed week, each vault writes a record of the referred stake present during that week in token seconds, split into the year one, year two and mature lanes, and a record of your own stake present during that week. The hub reads a week's referred average from that week's own record, never from a sample of another week, so a week is priced on what was actually present during it.

    A week's rate never falls. When another vault's record for the same week arrives later and raises the inputs, the week's rate rises and the hub tops up what it already paid for that week. Anyone can trigger that top up with settleWeeks, and our keeper does so after every rate rise. Until then a claimable balance may read low, never high.

    Your First Week: a Worked Example

    The rule is short. A week's commission is the week's time weighted referred average, times the tier rate that average reaches, times 7 over 364. The tier is read from the summed average of all your referees over that week (plus the tier your own stake reaches). A deposit counts from the second it lands, in token seconds, so a deposit made during a week is present for only the remaining fraction of that week. Weeks start on Thursday at 00:00 UTC, because the week number is the Unix timestamp divided by one week.

    • Example (a), one referee deposits exactly 5,000 on a Monday: the week began the previous Thursday at 00:00 UTC, so a deposit at Monday 00:00 UTC is present for the remaining three of seven days, about 43 percent of the week. The week's average is about 2,143, below the first rung of 5,000, so that week pays 0. Every following full week averages 5,000, reaches the first rung of 1 percent, and pays 5,000 times 1 percent times 7 over 364, about 0.96 USDC.
    • Example (b), one referee deposits 8,000 when 25 percent of the week has elapsed: the stake is present for 75 percent of the week, so the average is 6,000, above the first rung. That first week pays 6,000 times 1 percent times 7 over 364, about 1.15 USDC, and every following full week pays 8,000 times 1 percent times 7 over 364, about 1.54 USDC.
    • Example (c), the rung is on the sum: two referees at 3,000 each for a full week average 6,000 together and reach the first rung, although neither alone does. The week pays about 1.15 USDC.

    A partial first week that falls under the first rung pays nothing and carries nothing over; the week's average is what it was, and the next week starts fresh.

    Commission for a week becomes claimable only after the week closes, its crumb is flushed to the hub and the envelope is applied there. Until then the week is measured but not yet priced.

    Building and Losing Credit:

    Deposits and compounded interest build referred credit. Any movement that changes ownership or leaves the vault destroys it: a transfer, a withdrawal, a sale through the withdrawal queue (the join is the moment of intent to sell), and a burn. The destruction is permanent. Credit is never rebuilt by a transfer back, by a queue leave or by a purchase through the queue; only a new deposit or compound builds credit again.

    Two movements keep credit: a move between two vaults of the same product on the same chain, and a bridge to the same owner's address on another chain. In both cases the credit lands in the destination vault with exactly the age it had, so a relocation never makes a cohort younger or older. What a queue join does to your book, step by step, is in What if I Join the Exit Queue?.

    Shipping Weeks to the Hub:

    Nothing is paid on the vault. Each vault holds a book per referrer and ships it to the hub as a flush envelope. A flush is a transaction on the vault that settles the referrer's book, plans the closed weeks not yet shipped and sends them. From a vault on Base, Arbitrum or Robinhood the envelope travels to the hub over Chainlink CCIP; you pay the CCIP fee in the chain's native token and any excess is refunded. From a vault on mainnet the envelope is delivered to the hub in the same transaction by a direct call.

    • Who may flush: you may always flush your own book. Otherwise only the flush bots we allowlisted may flush a referrer's book. A courier can only carry your book on a vault after your own first flush there, and that first own flush opens the lane even when none of your weeks has closed yet. The vaults also carry a public flush switch; it ships off and our policy is an allowlist with one courier address per vault; a vault that is paused gets its courier only after its unpause and one own flush round.
    • The courier share: when an allowlisted bot delivers closed weeks that were not yet priced, the protocol pays the bot a courier share of 5 percent (500 basis points under the genesis table) of the commission those weeks earned. The share is paid on top of your commission, never deducted from it. A referrer flushing its own book earns no share, and a flush from any address that is not an allowlisted bot earns none either. We can bound the share per delivery with setDelivererShareCap.
    • Only closed weeks travel: the current week is never shipped, so nobody can gain by flushing at a particular moment inside a week. Each envelope carries at most 16 referrers and a bounded amount of work, so every accepted flush fits one transaction at the hub.

    Claiming Your Commission:

    Commission accrues at the hub in USDC into a claimable bucket per source chain and referrer: what you earned on Base is a different bucket from what you earned on mainnet. The hub is its own pot; it is funded by plain USDC transfers from the protocol and never draws on a vault.

    There are three claim calls on the hub.

    • claim(chainId, referrer): pays one bucket in full and reverts with PotDry when the pot holds less than the bucket.
    • claimUpTo(chainId, referrer, amount): pays up to the given amount and up to what the pot holds, and leaves the rest claimable.
    • claimAll(referrer, chainIds): pays every listed bucket that can pay, each up to the cover left after the buckets before it, and reverts with NothingClaimable only when it paid nothing at all.

    A claim can be sent by you or by the bucket's payout address, and the USDC always goes to the payout address.

    Your Payout Address per Chain:

    The hub chain bucket pays your own address by default, and you can set another payout address on the hub with setPayoutAddress. A spoke chain bucket (Base, Arbitrum, Robinhood) pays nothing until a designation from that chain has landed at the hub. Set the designation on that chain's registry with setPayoutAddress; the next flush of your book on that chain carries it to the hub, or push it at once with pushPayoutAddress, paying the CCIP fee. The newest designation wins. The reader view claimablesOf lists your buckets with the address each would pay, and a zero address there means the bucket is not claimable yet.

    A designation of the zero address is valid and pays the same address as your own on mainnet. If you are a contract wallet on a spoke chain, designate a nonzero mainnet address you control, because the same address on mainnet may be nobody's, or someone else's.

    If your referrer address is a smart wallet, make sure it exists on every chain your referees deposit on, because only that address can designate on that chain and we cannot designate for you. A wallet that exists on mainnet alone earns on Base into a bucket nobody can release until the same wallet is deployed at the same address on Base, which most smart wallets allow through the same factory and salt. The safe choice is to share an address you control everywhere, an externally owned account or a wallet deployed on every chain, and a good front end warns a depositor when a link carries a contract address that has no code on the deposit chain.

    What Should a Referrer Expect?

    What if the Pot Is Short?

    The pot is funded by the protocol. When it is short, claim reverts and claimUpTo or claimAll pay what is covered, in the order you list the buckets. Nothing is lost; the remainder stays claimable until the pot is topped up.

    Can a Priced Week Change?

    A closed week keeps its verdict. Its rate can rise when more records for it arrive, never fall, and no commission already credited is ever clawed back, not even after a correction of the referrer.

    What if a Week Is Flushed Late?

    Later delivery costs nothing except time. A week flushed late is priced at the rate it had when it closed, from its own record. The vaults keep up to 104 weeks of history per book for the first flush, so flush each book at least once every two years.

    Who Controls What Is Shipped?

    Your own flush is the one that advances the vault's shipping cursors. A courier can only ship weeks above your cursors, so if you want to control what is shipped first, flush your own book first.

    Does the Queue Cost Me Commission?

    Yes, from the join. The queued shares leave your book at the join, newest credit first, earn you nothing while queued, and do not come back when the referee leaves the queue unfilled; a new deposit is the only way credit returns.

    Does Stake on Different Chains Add Up?

    Everything is a sum across chains. Referred stake in one vault and own stake in another add up on the same ladder in the same week, and relocating stake between vaults does not change the age of its credit.

    Conclusion

    You earn a nominal yearly rate on the principal your referees keep in the vaults, counted in whole weeks at the rate of each week, with your own stake raising the tier and the cap at 10 percent. Closed weeks never lose their verdict, the courier share is paid on top, and your commission waits in a bucket per chain at the hub until you claim it in USDC.

    How it Works:

    The Elevated Referrer Address Rule:

    An integrator is identified by one thing only: the referrer address it writes into the referrer field of every deposit it sends. That exact address is what the registry records for each depositor and what the hub prices, so choose one address and use it on every chain and in every deposit. If you are to be elevated, that is priced at the cap for every week of your window (10 percent on year one credit, 5 percent on year two credit and 1 percent from year three on under the genesis table), tell us that address before your first deposit. We then grant the elevation through the hub lever: proposeLeverChange with the exact elevateReferrer(referrer, fromWeek) call, three days, then elevateReferrer for a week in the future. The grant is never retroactive: weeks before its from week are priced on the ladder, and a window that was ended keeps its verdict for good.

    Commission accrues per source chain and referrer at the hub, so an integrator earning on several chains holds one bucket per chain. A contract address used as referrer on Base, Arbitrum or Robinhood must designate a mainnet payout address with setPayoutAddress on that chain's registry and have it pushed to the hub (with the next flush of its book or with pushPayoutAddress), because the hub otherwise pays the referrer address itself on mainnet, where the same address may be undeployed or belong to someone else. On mainnet the bucket pays the referrer address by default and setPayoutAddress on the hub redirects it.

    Tell us the referrer address before the first deposit. An elevation granted later prices the earlier weeks on the ladder, and nothing is ever re priced.

    Several Vault Systems, One Referrer Address:

    The referral umbrella covers several vault systems: the nine World Mobile vaults are system 0 and the Sovren vault (wsnusdc on mainnet) is system 1, under one rate table, cap, ladder, courier share and payout pot. Use the same referrer address in every system; your ladder inputs sum over every vault of every system, so 500,000 referred in a World Mobile vault plus 500,000 in the Sovren vault counts as 1,000,000. The hub tags each source with its system number for reporting only, and the user facing functions of the Sovren vault are the same as those of a World Mobile vault, because it is built on the same code. Money never moves between systems, so a user cannot move or bridge between a Sovren vault and a World Mobile vault. The Sovren vault joins the referral system alongside the World Mobile rollout and takes no part until its join completes.

    Functions Reference

    This section lists every user facing function of a vault, grouped by what it does, with one sentence each on what it does, who may call it and what it changes.

    Entry

    deposit(uint256 _amount) pulls the underlying token from the caller, mints shares one to one and credits the cohort recorder; anyone may call it while deposits are not paused. depositAndClaimInterest(uint256 _amount) and depositAndCompoundInterest(uint256 _amount) do the same and settle the caller's pending interest in the same transaction, paid out or compounded into shares. depositWithPermit2(uint256 _amount, uint256 _nonce, uint256 _deadline, bytes _signature) and its two combined variants pull the token through the Permit2 contract with the caller's signature instead of an allowance. depositWithReferral(uint256 _amount, address _referrer), depositAndClaimInterestWithReferral, depositAndCompoundInterestWithReferral and the three ...WithPermit2WithReferral variants record the caller's referrer in the registry first (write once, zero and self refused) and then run the matching deposit. A deposit that raises the caller's balance by the grace threshold or more stamps the caller's grace lock. The grace period is switched off on every vault (gracePeriodDuration 0 since 2026-11-07): it was 45 days (3,888,000 seconds) before, off for one day on 2026-11-05 and restored on 2026-11-06, every change by the master without a timelock, so the value can change again at any time, and a stamped lock waits for the duration in force when the lock is checked, which at 0 means no wait at all. mintFromPeer(address _user, uint256 _amount) and ccipReceive are the destination ends of a move and a bridge and are callable only by a peer vault or the CCIP router.

    Balance

    balanceOf(address) and totalSupply() are the share token views; a share is one unit of the underlying. getPendingInterest(address _user), getPendingInterestBulk(address[] _users), getTotalInterestUser(address _user) and getTotalInterestUserBulk(address[] _users), with their ...ByTimeStamp variants, read the interest accrued and the total earned; interestRate() is the current rate; getTotalCashedInterest() is the sum of every settled interest bucket; getBridgeableBalance(address _user) and getMoveableBalance(address _user) read what a user may bridge or move. The recorder views getTrackedContribution, getAttributedContribution, getCohortBook and getCohortScore read the referral ledger; all of these change nothing.

    Compound

    compoundInterest() settles the caller's pending interest and mints it as shares; claimInterestPartiallyAndCompound(uint256 _amount) pays part and compounds the rest. compoundInterestOnBehalf(address _user) on AutoCompoundFacet does the same for a user who opted in with setAutoCompoundAllowed(true), is callable only by a master allowlisted bot, and keeps autoCompoundFeeBps of the compounded interest as the bot's reward in the underlying token; the net is minted to the user and credited to the recorder like a deposit. compoundInterestViaFulfillBulk compounds the caller's interest by buying queue orders with it.

    Claim

    On the vault, claimInterest() pays the caller's whole pending interest in the underlying token, claimInterestExactAmount(uint256 _amount) pays part of it, and moveMyInterestTo(uint256 _amount, address _target, bool _all) moves settled interest to another account's bucket; all three are callable by the account owner and wait for the grace period after a large deposit while a grace period is set. On the hub on mainnet, claim(uint256 _chainId, address _referrer), claimUpTo(uint256 _chainId, address _referrer, uint256 _amount) and claimAll(address _referrer, uint256[] _chainIds) pay a referrer's commission buckets in USDC to the bucket's payout address, callable by the referrer or that payout address, and lower claimable and raise totalClaimed.

    Exit Queue

    joinQue(uint256 _amount, int256 _incentive) escrows the caller's shares as an order at the chosen incentive and clamps them off any referrer's book; anyone with shares may call it while deposits are not paused. leaveQue(uint256 _queMemberId, int256 _incentive) returns an unfilled order's shares to the caller and reduceQueAmount(uint256 _queMemberId, int256 _incentive, uint256 _reduceBy) returns part of them, both callable by the order's owner at any time, without restoring referral credit. switchQueIncentive and switchQueIncentivePartial move an order to another incentive tier. On the buyer side fulfillOrder, partiallyFulfillOrder and fulfillOrderBulk pay the underlying token for queued shares, callable by anyone. The views getAllOrdersfromAddressWithId, getAllOrdersOverallWithId, solveForAmount, getFulfillmentPlanForIncentive, predictCostForTokens, predictTokensForCost, predictDiscountedAmount and solveForAmountAfterFulfill price and list orders and change nothing.

    Relocation

    moveBetweenVaults(address _dstVault, uint256 _amount) burns the caller's shares and mints them in a peer vault of the same product on the same chain, carrying the referral attribution; bridgeToVault(uint64 _destChainSelector, uint256 _amount) and bridgeToVaultWithReferral burn them and send a CCIP message that mints them to the same address on the destination chain, with the attribution attached by the vault itself; quoteBridgeFee and quoteBridgeFeeWithReferral quote the native fee. Both relocations wait for the grace period after a large deposit while a grace period is set.

    Referral

    flushReferrals(address[] _referrers) and flushReferralWindow(address _referrer, uint256 _fromWeek, uint256 _fromGapIndex) ship a referrer's closed weeks to the hub, callable by the referrer for its own book and by allowlisted flush bots for any book, and advance the referrer's send cursors only on an own flush. syncReferee(address _user) and advanceBook(address _referrer, uint256 _maxBoundaries) are permissionless pokes that clamp a user and pre walk a book. On the registry, setPayoutAddress(address _payout), pushPayoutAddress(address _owner), broadcastSync(address _user, uint64[] _selectors), pokeMigration(address _user) and cancelCorrectReferrer(address _user) are the user and public calls described in UI Integration.

    Admin Functions and What the Referral Upgrade Changes

    The master functions of a vault as they stand are these.

    • On the diamond itself: proposeSelector, proposeSelectors, executeSelectorChange, executeSelectorChanges and cancelSelectorProposal route selectors to facets behind a three day timelock after finalizeSetup.
    • On AdminFacet: pauseDeposits and unpauseDeposits; mintSupply(address _to, uint256 _amount) and burnSupply(address _from, uint256 _amount), and the one way disAllowSupplyChangeByOwner() that renounces both (and setCashedInterest with them) for good, which has not been called; setInterestRate, setTotalDepositCap, setDepositsDisabled, setGracePeriodDuration, setGraceThresholdAmount, setGraceFreezeEnabled, setDepositAccumWindow, setSweeper and setWiseToken; the three day propose, execute and cancel trios for the third party address, the worker address, the transfer hook facet, the deposit hook facet, the referral registry pointer and the flush hub pointer; and setCohortSyncEnabled, the instant arming switch of the recorder.
    • On InterestAdminFacet: setCashedInterest(address _user, uint256 _amount).
    • On BridgeFacet: the one shot setCcipRouter, setReferralEnabled, setBridgeGasLimit, and the timelocked peer trio with the instant removeCrossChainPeer.
    • On MoveFacet: the timelocked peer vault trio with the instant removePeerVault.
    • On QueueAdminFacet: changeMinDepositAmount and setNegativeIncentivesNotAllowed.
    • On AutoCompoundFacet: setAutoCompoundBot and setAutoCompoundFeeBps.
    • On RescueFacet: rescueToken.
    • On CohortFlushPolicyFacet: setAllowedFlushBot, setPublicFlushEnabled and setFlushGasLimit.
    • On CohortRecorderFacet: seedCohortContribution, which dies with the registry's seed latch.

    The referral upgrade revokes no admin function. It adds timelocked selector routes for the referral, recorder, flush and flush policy facets, installs CohortHookFacet as the transfer hook through the existing hook timelock, and points the vault at its registry and at the hub through two new pointer trios with the same three day delay. mintSupply and burnSupply stay master powers unless the one way disAllowSupplyChangeByOwner is called. At the time of writing the vault master is the deployer key; it moves to a multisig as the last step of the rollout.

    The registry master levers are setAllowedVault (instant before finalizeSyncSetup and for vaults ever allowed; a new address takes proposeAllowedVault, three days, executeAllowedVaultChange), proposeSyncPeer, executeSyncPeerChange, cancelSyncPeerChange and the instant removeSyncPeer, the one shot setCcipRouter and the three day proposeCcipRouter and executeCcipRouterChange, proposePayoutHub and executePayoutHubChange, setSyncGasLimit, raiseMaxSyncRank, finalizeSyncSetup, the correction lane proposeCorrectReferrer and executeCorrectReferrer with its three day window, and the one way latchCorrections and latchSeeds. The hub master levers are the selector trio behind three days after finalizeSetup, proposeLeverChange and cancelLeverChange, and the setters listed in Timelocks and Master Levers, every grant waiting three days and every revocation immediate. On the registry and the hub the master is a multisig from the deploy.

    Pre Audit Notes

    • Nothing deployed is edited: the referral system is added to the vault diamonds as new facets routed through the three day selector timelock, and the transfer hook is swapped through its own three day timelock. The storage rules that make this safe are in Storage Rules.
    • Every write path is permissionless and idempotent, or master only behind three days: the hub's applyFlush and settleWeeks, the registry's broadcastSync and pokeMigration and the vault's syncReferee and advanceBook can be called by anyone and change nothing when called twice; every grant waits three days and is public from the moment it is queued.
    • Master can never write money: we can never write a score, a cursor, a week bucket or a claimable on the hub, can never change a referrer alone on the registry, and can never move a user's recorded stake on a vault except through the consent gated correction lane and the retroactive seed.
    • Every loop has a constant bound next to it: 104 week boundaries per settle, 52 ring slots, 16 entries and 160 work units per envelope (6 and 96 on the hub chain), 104 weeks per settleWeeks call, 8 tracked vaults per registry, 8 elevation windows per referrer, 520 weeks of schedule horizon.
    • The accepted findings are published: every review finding that was accepted rather than fixed is listed with its mechanism and reason in Safety and Known Findings.

    Conclusion

    Use one referrer address in every deposit you send, tell us that address before the first deposit if you are to be elevated, and designate a mainnet payout address for any contract referrer on a spoke chain. Your reviewers will find that the upgrade adds timelocked routes and revokes nothing, that master cannot touch commission, and that every accepted finding is written down with its reason.

    Part Two: Referral UI Integration Guide

    Link: Referral System, UI Integration Guide

    The referral system lets a depositor name a referrer with a deposit and pays that referrer a weekly commission at the hub on mainnet. Your front end has four jobs: pass the referrer into the deposit, show a user their referrer, show a referrer their standing and claimables with the claim buttons, and let a user veto a correction. Everything else is reading views and listening to events; the reference tables at the end of this chapter list every one of them.

    deposit with referrer  ->  the registry records the pair, once
every closed week      ->  the vault writes the referrer's record
shipment               ->  closed weeks reach the hub (CCIP from Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood; a direct call on mainnet)
hub                    ->  prices the weeks, credits a claimable bucket per source chain and referrer
claim                  ->  USDC paid on mainnet to the payout address

    The chain ids the hub keys its buckets by are Ethereum 1, Base 8453, Arbitrum 42161 and Robinhood Chain 4663. The examples below use web3.js; ZERO is the zero address, CHAINS is [1, 8453, 42161, 4663], and the contract addresses come from Addresses.

    Flushing is described, not integrated, for now. Between the vault's weekly record and the hub's pricing sits the flush: a transaction on the vault that settles a referrer's book and ships its closed weeks to the hub, over CCIP from Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood and by a direct call on mainnet. Shipping Weeks to the Hub explains it, including who may flush and the courier share. A front end does not need a flush button at the start: the protocol's couriers ship every book on a schedule, so a referrer's weeks reach the hub and become claimable without any action in the user interface. The flush calls and their errors join this chapter once a flush button becomes part of the integration; the two cursor views a front end reads for the activation state are already in the reference table and in Which Views for Which Screen.

    The referral deposit entrypoints on the vault, all on the user's chain, are these.

    The Payout Designation:

    Link: Payout Designation, UI Integration Guide

    Commission accrues at the hub per source chain and referrer. On mainnet a referrer's bucket pays the referrer's own address unless the referrer sets another with setPayoutAddress(address _payout) on the hub. On Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood a bucket pays nothing until a designation from that chain has reached the hub.

    On the spoke chain's registry:

    setPayoutAddress(address _payout)
pushPayoutAddress(address _owner) payable
quotePushPayoutFee(address _owner) returns (uint256)
getPayoutDesignation(address _owner) returns (address payout, uint256 stamp)

    await registry.methods.setPayoutAddress(mainnetPayout).send({ from: referrer });

const pushFee = await registry.methods.quotePushPayoutFee(referrer).call();
await registry.methods.pushPayoutAddress(referrer).send({ from: referrer, value: pushFee });

    setPayoutAddress records the designation under the caller's address; a second designation in the same second reverts, so a correction waits one block. The designation travels to the hub with the next shipment of the referrer's book on that chain, or at once through pushPayoutAddress with the quoted CCIP fee as value, which anyone may send for any owner. The newest designation wins at the hub. Offer the push button when claimablesOf shows a zero payout for a chain with a nonzero amount.

    A designation of the zero address is valid and pays the referrer's own address on mainnet. A referrer that is a contract wallet on a spoke chain must designate a nonzero mainnet address it controls. A vault address can never designate.

    The reverse case has no shortcut. A contract wallet that exists on mainnet alone cannot designate on a spoke chain, because nobody can sign for that address there, and nothing on the hub or the registry lets us designate on its behalf. Its spoke buckets stay unclaimable until the same wallet is deployed at the same address on that chain (a Safe or an account factory with the same owners and salt lands on the same address) and signs the designation there; the commission stays in the pot in the meantime, lost to nobody else. The check in The Referral Link keeps a front end from creating that case.

await hub.methods.claimAll(referrer, claimableChains).send({ from: referrer });
    • claim: pays one bucket in full and reverts PotDry when the pot holds less.
    • claimUpTo: pays up to the given amount and up to the pot's balance.
    • claimAll: pays every listed bucket that can pay, in list order against the pot's cover, and reverts NothingClaimable only when nothing was paid.

    A claim may be sent by the referrer or by the bucket's payout address and always pays the payout address. Show claimable per chain, the hub's USDC balance as the cover, and offer claimAll with the chains that have a nonzero amount and a nonzero payoutAddressOf.

    Errors:

    From the vault, deposit with referrer:

    Error Cause
    InvalidValue The referrer is the zero address.
    SelfReferral The referrer is the depositor itself.
    ReferralRegistryNotSet The vault's registry pointer is not set yet.
    InvalidReferrer (from the registry) The referrer is a vault, the registry, the hub, or an address whose own referral chain leads back to the depositor.

    The plain deposit errors apply as well: DepositsDisabled, DepositExceedCap, the pause, and for the combined variants GracePeriodNotElapsed and NoInterest.

    From the registry, designation and veto:

    Error Cause

    PayoutStampNotNewer A second designation in the same second; wait one block.
    NothingToPush No designation to push for that owner.
    InsufficientPushFee The value sent is below quotePushPayoutFee.
    NoCorrectionProposed No proposal is open against the user.
    NotCorrectionParty The caller is neither the user nor the operator.

    From the hub, claims:

    Error Cause
    PotDry claim asked for more than the pot holds; use claimUpTo or claimAll.
    NothingClaimable claimAll paid nothing.
    NotPayoutAuthorized The caller is neither the referrer nor the bucket's payout address.
    PayoutNotDesignated A spoke chain bucket has no designation at the hub yet.

    Gotchas:

    • The referrer is the address in the field, nothing else: an integrator that wants to be the referrer of every deposit it sends writes its own address there, on every chain, in every deposit.
    • The first assignment wins everywhere: a later deposit with another referrer succeeds as a deposit and the argument is ignored; show the recorded referrer, not the link's.
    • Spoke chain buckets need a designation: without one the bucket accrues but cannot be claimed, and a contract wallet on a spoke chain must designate a mainnet address it controls.
    • A contract referrer must exist on the deposit chain: a spoke bucket is released only by a designation that address signs on that chain, so a smart wallet that exists on mainnet alone cannot claim what it earns on Base, Arbitrum or Robinhood until it is deployed at the same address there; read the referrer's code on the deposit chain and warn or refuse the link.
    • The current week is never shipped: only closed weeks travel, and a week becomes claimable only after its envelope is applied at the hub.
    • A closed week keeps its verdict: rates can rise when more records arrive, never fall, and nothing already credited is clawed back.
    • Joining the exit queue ends the referrer's credit for the queued amount, like a withdrawal would; leaving the queue unfilled restores nothing.

    Which Views for Which Screen

    • A depositor's profile: read getReferrerPair on the user's chain for the referrer and the time since when, proposedCorrection and correctionQueuedAt for an open proposal to veto, and getAttributedContribution on each vault for how much of the user's stake is credited.
    • A referrer's overview: read claimablesOf on the reader for accrued and claimable per chain, ladderStandingOf for the current tier and rates, ladderInputsRange and weeklyRatesRange for the week charts, and getPayoutDesignation on each spoke registry beside payoutAddressOf on the hub for the designation state.
    • A referrer's activation: three states, each cleared by one action of the referrer. A vault needs the referrer's first own flush when getFlushedThroughWeek on that vault reads zero while the fourth value of getCohortBook, the last settle timestamp, is nonzero: the book exists but its owner has never shipped it, and a courier cannot carry it until that first own flush. A spoke chain needs a designation when getPayoutDesignation on its registry returns a stamp of zero; the hub ignores an unset designation, so a flush does not stand in for setPayoutAddress. A designation is set but not landed when the registry's stamp is above payoutStamp(chainId, owner) on the hub for that chain; the bucket stays unclaimable, and claimablesOf shows a zero payout for it, until the designation reaches the hub with the next flush of the book or a push. Mainnet needs no designation.
    • A per vault detail: read getCohortBook and getCohortWeekCrumbs for the measured weeks and weekReportsAtSource with paidCursorsAtSource on the reader for shipped versus settled weeks.
    • An indexer: build the history from ReferralAssigned, CohortContributionCredited, CrumbPriced, GapPriced, WeekToppedUp, PayoutAddressSet, PayoutClaimed, the correction events and the elevation events.

    Batching the spoke activation. The vault's MulticallFacet cannot do it: multicall batches calls into the same diamond only and is not payable, so it can neither reach the registry nor carry the CCIP fee of the flush. Spoke activation is a wallet level batch, EIP 5792 calls from a smart wallet or an externally owned account under EIP 7702, with the registry designation first, so that the flush carries it, and the vault flush with its fee second, or two transactions in that order. The flush refunds the excess fee to the sender, so a contract wallet that sends it must be able to receive the native token.

    Conclusion

    Pass the referrer address into the deposit, read the registry for the user's referrer, read the reader for the referrer's standing and claimables, and offer the designation, claim and veto buttons on the chain each call belongs to. The reference tables give you every view and every event with its signature, so a dashboard and an indexer can show the whole picture without guessing at anything.

    Architecture for Developers

    The components, the data flow and the rules that keep every chain in agreement.

    The referral system is three sets of contracts around the running vault diamonds: new facets on every vault, one registry per chain and one hub on Ethereum mainnet. This chapter describes each component, the path a deposit takes until it becomes a claim, the rules of the registry, the bounds on every loop, the levers we hold and the storage discipline. Paths are relative to the repository root. The registries, the hub and the reader are live and verified on every chain, and the vault facets are deployed with their selector routes queued on all nine vaults behind the three day timelock. A source file leaves its pending/ folder only after the broadcast that routes it is verified, so some paths below still carry pending/ while the routes wait.

    The signature of every view and event is in UI Integration; the user facing functions are in For Integrators and Partners.

    The Components

    The Vault Diamonds and the Cohort Facets

    Every Wise Telecom Nodes vault is a selector routed diamond. The referral system adds facets to it; nothing already deployed is edited. The new facets live in src/diamond/vault/facets/.

    • ReferralFacet and Permit2ReferralFacet: the referral variants of the deposit entrypoints. Their shared step, in ReferralFacetBase, forwards the pair (msg.sender, referrer) to the chain's registry through the vault's registry pointer and lets the registry apply the write once rule. getReferrerOf reads the registry through the same pointer.
    • The cohort recorder: the engine that measures stake, compiled into three engine slices that execute through the vault's transfer hook pointer and the dispatcher. CohortHookFacet is the transfer hook slice, installed as the vault's transferHookFacet, carrying the sync, credit, advance and settle entrypoints; CohortMoveHookFacet is the same chain move carry; CohortBridgeHookFacet is the bridge carry and the referrer flip. They share the bases CohortRecorderBase, CohortClampBase, CohortLaneBase, CohortBookBase and CohortMoveCarryBase, and CohortPinnedBase pins the genesis storage slots they touch so the slices stay small. The engine never reverts inside the transfer hook: lanes saturate and report through events.
    • CohortRecorderFacet: the routed public surface of the recorder: the permissionless clamp poke syncReferee, the permissionless bounded pre walk advanceBook, the registry only receiveReferrerFlip, the master only seedCohortContribution and the read views.
    • CohortFlushFacet with CohortFlushPlanner and CohortFlushTransport: ships the books to the hub. CohortFlushPolicyFacet holds the flush bot allowlist, the public flush switch and the CCIP gas limit of the flush message, with their read views.
    • AdminFacet: carries the pointer to the hub and its three day change timelock (proposeFlushHub, executeFlushHubChange, cancelFlushHubChange, getFlushHub), the registry pointer and the arming switch setCohortSyncEnabled.

    The Shared Libraries

    src/diamond/shared/CohortWeeks.sol holds the week epoch, the lane geometry and the carry codecs that the vault facets and the hub share, so no contract restates these numbers. The week is block.timestamp / 1 weeks; the ring has 52 slots; the year is 52 weeks; a book word and a crumb word hold three 80 bit lanes (year one, year two, mature); a crumb word also sets bit 252 when written and carries CRUMB_VERSION 1.

    src/diamond/shared/CohortFlushCodec.sol defines the flush envelope: a packed sequence of 32 byte words without ABI framing, FLUSH_VERSION 2, at most MAX_FLUSH_ENTRIES (16) entries and MAX_FLUSH_WORK (160) work units, a crumb, a self week and a gap week costing one unit each.

    The Registry

    src/registry/ReferralRegistry.sol is the per chain referrer registry, one standalone contract per chain, built from ReferralRegistryDeclarations (storage, events, errors), ReferralRegistryHelper (the checks, the merge and the CCIP transport) and ReferralRegistryAdmin (the master lanes). Every vault on the chain reads and writes the same registry. The registry is its own CCIP endpoint for the referrer sync between chains and for the payout designation push to the hub, it stages the same chain move carry between two vaults, and it holds the correction lane.

    The Hub

    src/hub/ReferralHub.sol is the mainnet hub, a diamond of its own with five facets that share the ReferralHubDeclarations layout.

    • ReferralHubCheckpointsFacet: the week bucket reads (ladderInputsAt, weekReportOf, weekSelfOf).
    • ReferralHubRatesFacet: the rate reads and the master levers pushRateTable, setDelivererShareCap, elevateReferrer and endElevation.
    • ReferralHubIntakeFacet: the reporter surface for the mainnet vaults (reportSelf, deliverCrumbs, deliverGap), the permissionless settleWeeks, the source and cursor reads, and the levers setAllowedReporter and setSourceSystem.
    • ReferralHubMailboxFacet: the CCIP receive, the permissionless applyFlush, and the levers setCcipRouter, setFlushSource and setDesignationSource.
    • ReferralHubPayoutFacet: the claim rail (claim, claimUpTo, claimAll), the hub chain setPayoutAddress and payoutAddressOf.

    The entry contract holds the constructor (which seeds the genesis rate table), the two step ownership, the selector timelock, the lever change proposals and the fallback dispatcher. The internal chain ReferralHubDeclarations to ReferralHubCheckpoints to ReferralHubRates to ReferralHubIntake to ReferralHubMailbox is compiled into the facets that need it. IReferralHub is the external surface of the deployed diamond. ReferralHubReader is a stateless read companion deployed as a second unit; it reads only the hub's primitive getters, so it can be redeployed at any time.

    How it Works:

    From a Deposit to a Claim:

    1. The deposit names a referrer. A depositor calls a referral deposit entrypoint. The facet calls assignReferrer on the registry. The registry refuses a zero or self referrer, the registry itself, the hub, any address ever allowed as a vault and any referrer whose chain leads back to the user, writes referrerOf[user] once, stamps referrerKey[user] and emits ReferralAssigned. A user who already has a referrer is silently kept. The deposit then runs as a plain deposit.
    2. The deposit credits the recorder. The transfer hook slice resolves the user's referrer through a guarded staticcall to the registry (effectiveReferrerOf, which answers the old referrer while a flip is pending), raises the user's trackedContribution, and credits the referrer's book: the current slot of the year one ring and the book's year one lane, plus the user's attributedContribution. A deposit without a referrer tracks but attributes nothing; the own stake still feeds the self input.
    3. Every later touchpoint settles first. A settle walks the week boundaries crossed since the last settle, at most 104 per call, rotates the rings at each boundary (year one into year two, year two into mature), writes one crumb per completed week into cohortWeekLog, and compresses a dormant tail into a gap record in cohortGapLog. The referrer's own stake is settled into a per account self crumb, and every completed week appends a self log record.
    4. Movements clamp. A transfer, a withdrawal, a queue join or a burn runs the one way clamp: the user's tracked contribution falls to the balance held, and the referrer's book is charged at most min(deficit, attributed) through the sale walk, newest first through both rings and then the mature lane. A sender the hook cannot clamp in place is parked in one slot and drained at the next entrypoint. A same chain move captures the taken ring slots as pairs, stages them in the registry (stageCarry) and the destination consumes them in the same transaction (consumeCarry); a bridge packs the pairs into the bridge message and the destination lands them under its own registry's answer. Every carried pair lands at the slot of its own credit week, aged only by the week boundaries crossed in flight.
    5. A flush ships closed weeks. flushReferrals or flushReferralWindow settles the book, plans the entries from storage (closed weeks only: the crumb of each week, the week's own self presence as runs of equal weekly averages, gap slices for dormant spans, and the referrer's payout designation read from the registry in CCIP mode) and sends one envelope. On a spoke chain the envelope goes to the hub over CCIP; on mainnet the facet calls the hub's reporter surface directly, entry by entry.
    6. The hub prices. The mailbox stores a commitment for every received envelope and anyone applies it with applyFlush. Each entry reports its self runs, its crumbs and its gap weeks into the week buckets, prices each (source, referrer, week) cell against its cumulative cursor at the week's current rates, credits the referrer's claimable bucket for the source's chain, and pays the courier share when a deliverer is set.
    7. The referrer claims. claim, claimUpTo or claimAll transfer USDC from the hub's own balance to the bucket's payout address.

    The Registry: Keys and the Merge Rule

    A sync key is (assignedAt << 8) | originChainRank. Each registry is constructed with its chain's rank (mainnet 0, Base 1, Arbitrum 2, Robinhood 3) and stamps every local assignment with the current timestamp and that rank. The lower key wins a merge; a lower rank wins a tie.

    Keys travel two ways. broadcastSync(user, chainSelectors) is permissionless and caller paid: it sends the user's current pair and key to the listed peer registries over CCIP. A bridge of the user's shares carries the origin key in the bridge carry payload, and the destination vault merges it into its registry through mergeReferrer.

    _mergeReferrerPair is the only place a referrer changes without the user's deposit. It refuses a key whose timestamp is more than one hour ahead of the local clock, whose timestamp mirrored against the local clock falls below SYNC_KEY_MIRROR, or whose rank is above maxSyncRank (three at construction, raised only by master before a fifth chain is peered). A user with no local key adopts the incoming pair. An incoming key equal to or above the local key changes nothing; an equal key naming a different referrer only emits SyncKeyCollision. A strictly lower key with the same referrer lowers the stored key. A strictly lower key with a different referrer flips the user: the registry rewrites referrerOf, marks a pending flip on every tracked vault that holds attributed contribution for the user, and emits ReferralSyncFlipped. A mutual pair resolves by key as well: the smaller key dissolves the losing edge, adopts the winner and drops any pending correction proposal of the dissolved user.

    A flip is delivered lazily. effectiveReferrerOf(user, vault) answers the old referrer for a vault with a pending flip marker, so every interim clamp and credit hits the book that holds the cover. The permissionless pokeMigration(user) (or pokeMigrationAt(user, vault) for one vault) calls receiveReferrerFlip on each marked vault: the vault settles the old book, charges the user's attributed slice out of it and replants its pairs at their own slots in the new referrer's rings. A disarmed vault defers the flip and keeps the marker; a vault without a book for the user consumes it.

    A correction mints a mirrored key ((SYNC_KEY_MIRROR - block.timestamp) << 8) | rank, which sorts below every genuine key, so a later correction sorts below an earlier one and a corrected pair wins every merge against every copy of the old pair. One broadcastSync after the execute carries it to every peer. The merge floor admits every genuine key and every correction executed at most one hour ahead of the local clock. A proposal is bound to the exact key held when it was made (proposedCorrectionKey), so any flip, key lowering or first assignment during the window makes the execute revert with NothingToCorrect. A correction of an existing pair must be proposed on the chain whose rank the held key carries (WrongCorrectionChain elsewhere); an assignment of a user without a referrer may be proposed on any chain. latchCorrections is a manual one way close of the lane; latchSeeds is the one way close of the retroactive seed, separate from it.

    Allowed vaults. Before finalizeSyncSetup we allow a vault instantly with setAllowedVault; afterwards a new address takes proposeAllowedVault, three days, executeAllowedVaultChange, and re allowing a vault the registry ever allowed stays instant. A revoke is instant and never removes the vault from the tracked list, which is monotone and holds at most MAX_TRACKED_VAULTS (8) addresses, because every vault that ever held book credit must keep receiving flip markers. The ever allowed flag also bars a vault from ever being a referrer or designating a payout. The CCIP router and every sync peer change through the same propose, three day, execute shape once the sync setup is finalized; removeSyncPeer is instant, cancels any pending proposal for that lane and raises the lane's quarantine flag, which the vaults read before merging a bridge carried pair from that chain.

    The Vault Flush: Cursors and Work Caps

    Each referrer has two send cursors on each vault. flushedThroughWeek is the last closed week whose crumb was shipped, and flushGapCursor packs the index of the next gap record to ship in its low 128 bits and the week at which to resume inside that record in its high 128 bits. Only the referrer's own flush writes the cursors, and they only advance. A third party (an allowlisted bot) ships weeks above both stored cursors, must wait for the referrer's first own flush before it can touch a virgin book, and is refused during the one day outage grace after a vault pause is lifted or the recorder is re armed. CohortReferrerFlushed carries the cursor values after every own flush.

    A virgin cursor anchors at the current week minus 103, so the first flush covers at most the last 104 closed weeks; advanceBook is the permissionless way to pre walk a dormant book before that. A batch of more than one referrer takes only books whose settle crosses no week boundary (FlushBookNotAdvanced otherwise), so one call never walks more than one book's boundaries. A single referrer settles inline.

    The planner works within the envelope caps: 16 entries, 160 work units on a spoke, and on the hub chain LOCAL_FLUSH_ENTRIES (6) and LOCAL_FLUSH_WORK (96), because that transaction also pays the hub intake. GAP_WORK_RESERVE (40) units are held back for the gap lane so a long dormant span keeps making progress beside the weekly crumbs. The deliverer packed into the header is the caller when the caller is an allowlisted flush bot, otherwise zero in a CCIP envelope and the referrer itself on the hub chain, whose intake refuses a zero deliverer. The CCIP message carries flushGasLimit (default 300,000, at most 2,000,000) and the fee is paid by the caller with the excess refunded. quoteFlushFee and previewFlushEnvelope are no settle lower bounds for planning.

    The Hub: Buckets per Source and Referrer

    Sources are keyed by bytes32. A local reporter is bytes32(uint256(uint160(reporter))); a remote source is keccak256(abi.encode(chainId, vault)) stamped by the mailbox from the message's authenticated origin. sourceKeyOf(chainId, vault) computes both. A source's chain id is written at its first delivery (sourceChainIdOf) and keys the claimable bucket the source credits.

    For every source, referrer and closed week the hub keeps sourceWeekOf (the reported referred average in the low half, the commission units already paid in the high half), sourceSelfOf (the reported self average) and paidCursor (the three lane cumulative quantity already priced). weekInputsOf[referrer][week] sums the referred averages in its low half and the self averages in its high half over every source; those two sums are the ladder inputs of the week. Every report for a closed week is final and merged by maximum, so a bucket only rises and a week's rates never fall.

    A delivery raises the week's bucket if its average is higher, then prices the increment of each lane above the cursor at the week's current rates: due is floor((y1 * y1Bps + y2 * y2Bps + m * mBps) / (10,000 * YEAR)) over the cumulative cursor, the cell is paid due - paidSoFar, and the referrer's bucket for the source's chain rises by it. A report that raises a week's rates emits WeekRatesRaised and tops up its own source's cell inline; every other source already paid for that week is topped up by the permissionless, idempotent settleWeeks(referrer, fromWeek, weekCount) (at most 104 weeks per call), so the settled price of a week never depends on the order its sources arrive in. A lane below its cursor emits CrumbLaneRegression and pays nothing.

    The courier fee is botShareBps of the delivered increment's own due, taken from the rate table in force for the week, clamped by delivererShareCap when that cap is nonzero, credited to the deliverer's bucket for the delivery's chain and counted in totalAccrued beside the commission. It is paid only when the deliverer is set and differs from the referrer, never on a top up.

    A bucket counts toward totalPayable, the amount the pot owes, only once its owner has a claim path: a hub chain bucket from setPayoutAddress or its owner's first claim, a spoke bucket from its first landed designation (PayoutBacklogReleased when the accrued backlog is released). totalPayable - totalClaimed is the outstanding amount the pot should hold. claim pays a bucket in full and reverts PotDry when the hub holds less; claimUpTo and claimAll pay up to the hub's balance. A spoke bucket without a landed designation reverts PayoutNotDesignated and claimAll skips it.

    The mailbox stores flushCommitment[messageId] as the hash of the source chain selector, chain id, vault and payload at receive time, and applyFlush re checks that commitment, that the source is still registered, the header versions, the entry count and, after the entries, the payload length and the work cap. A malformed envelope reverts as a whole and stays committed; a bad designation word is skipped with FlushDesignationSkipped and the entry still prices. A designation push from a registered registry is applied with the same latest wins rule as a piggybacked designation: a stamp (setAt << 8) | rank must exceed the stored one, and a stamp more than one hour ahead of the hub clock is skipped.

    Timelocks and Master Levers

    Every lane that lets a new party write is behind three days. On the vaults the selector routing, the transfer hook pointer, the registry pointer and the flush hub pointer each have their own propose, execute and cancel trio with SELECTOR_CHANGE_DELAY (3 days), instant before the vault's setup is finalized. On the registry the sync peers, the router, the payout hub route and new allowed vaults wait three days once finalizeSyncSetup ran; the correction lane waits three days by construction. On the hub, selector changes wait three days after finalizeSetup, and every grant waits the same delay through the lever timelock: proposeLeverChange(bytes call) records the hash of the exact setter call, LeverChangeProposed carries the full call so the pending grant is public for the whole delay, and the setter runs only as that exact call at least three days later, once. Grants are allowing a reporter, setting the router, setting a flush or designation source to a chain id and elevating a referrer. Revocations never wait: a reporter set to false, a source set to chain id zero, an elevation ended.

    The master levers of the hub are these.

    • pushRateTable(table): appends a table for a future week, strictly after the last table's week and within 520 weeks of now. A table needs a cap within 10,000 basis points, a year two rate within the cap, a mature rate within the year two rate, tiers that never fall from one rung to the next, strictly rising positive thresholds and a courier share within MAX_BOT_SHARE_BPS (2,000). Past weeks keep their table.
    • setDelivererShareCap(cap): bounds the courier fee of each priced cell in USD units; zero means no cap and is the genesis value.
    • elevateReferrer(referrer, fromWeek): opens an elevation window at a future week, open ended, as a grant behind the lever timelock. It refuses a referrer whose latest window is still open and a referrer whose history already holds MAX_ELEVATION_WINDOWS (8) windows (ElevationHistoryFull).
    • endElevation(referrer, untilWeek): closes the open window at a future week at or after its start, at once. A new grant after an end appends a window instead of replacing the ended one, so every window keeps its verdict for good; elevatedFromWeek and elevatedUntilWeek mirror the latest window, and ReferrerElevated and ReferrerElevationEnded carry each window's bounds.
    • setAllowedReporter(reporter, allowed): names a hub chain vault as a reporter (the grant waits, the revoke does not).
    • setCcipRouter, setFlushSource(chainSelector, vault, chainId) and setDesignationSource(chainSelector, registry, chainId): wire the CCIP side. A source is never set to the hub's own chain id, is never re keyed to another chain id without a removal first, and chain id zero removes it.
    • setSourceSystem(source, systemId): tags a source with its vault system (below MAX_SYSTEMS, 8) for indexers and the umbrella's accounting; pricing does not read it and the tag freezes once the source has delivered.

    We can never write a score, a cursor, a week bucket or a claimable on the hub, can never change a referrer alone on the registry, and can never move a user's recorded stake on a vault except through the consent gated correction lane and the retroactive seed, which credits only a user's untracked balance from the seed instant on and dies with the registry's seed latch.

    The vault and registry master functions, and what the upgrade changes among them, are listed in Admin Functions and What the Referral Upgrade Changes.

    Storage Rules

    The vaults are selector routed diamonds with one inherited declaration chain shared byte for byte by the entry contract and every facet. New state is only ever appended as a tail shard; nothing is removed, reordered or retyped, and there are no gap reserves. The referral and cohort shards occupy slots 67 to 95 of the vault layout: the dormant in diamond referrerOf (67), cohortSyncEnabled and trackedContribution (68, 69), the book, settle stamp, two rings, week log and gap log (70 to 75), a retired payout accumulator (76), the registry pointer trio (77 to 79), a retired hub pointer trio (80 to 82), the flush shard (83 to 89), attributedContribution (90), the divest park (91), the self crumb (92), the disarm span and outage stamps (93, 94) and the self log (95). Every shard follows the default off convention: a live diamond with zeroed tail storage behaves exactly as before until we arm the feature. The full table, the dated prose and the regeneration command are in src/diamond/STORAGE_LAYOUT.md; script/check_storage_layout.sh proves in CI that the diamond and every facet compile to the same layout and that no slot or byte has two owners.

    The hub is outside that shared chain: it is never delegatecalled by a vault and shares no storage with one. Its own layout, 40 entries from master at slot 0 to elevationWindowsOf at slot 39, is checked by the same script against test/hub/storage_snapshot/hub_layout.json, and it grows only at its tail; state that later designs retired stays declared and unused. The registry owns its layout outright (slots 0 to 29, listed in src/registry/README.md) and is never repointed by a vault once the recorder is armed.

    Safety and Known Findings

    Who can do what, what is behind a timelock, and every accepted review finding with its reason.

    The referral code was reviewed many times before its first deployment. Every finding was either fixed or accepted with a reason. This chapter starts with the trust model, so you know who can do what and what waits three days, and then lists the accepted findings grouped by the question you would ask: can commission be lost, can it be paid twice, can a bot take my share, can someone take my claim, what can the operator do, and what about the year, the thresholds and the reads. Each entry states the finding plainly, names the mechanism in the code and gives the reason it is not a problem: a documented design choice, a trusted role, an effect bounded to dust or to time, or an operator procedure that covers it.

    The Trust Model

    Who Can Do What?

    • Master: the protocol operator, a multisig from the deploy on the registry and the hub, and the only party that can grant anything. We can never write a score, a cursor, a week bucket or a claimable on the hub, can never change a referrer alone on the registry, and can never move a user's recorded stake on a vault except through the consent gated correction lane and the retroactive seed. At the time of writing the vault master is the deployer key; it moves to a multisig as the last step of the rollout.
    • A registered source: a vault we wired into the hub as a reporter or as a CCIP flush source; its code builds every number it sends from its own storage. Every number the hub takes comes from a registered source, which is the system's standing trust boundary.
    • An allowlisted flush bot: an address a vault's master allowed to flush other referrers' books; our policy is an allowlist with one courier address per vault and the public flush switch off.
    • The keeper: our offchain process that pokes the permissionless settlement calls.
    • Everyone: may deposit with a referrer, flush their own book, broadcast a referrer pair, apply a received envelope, settle weeks, poke a migration and claim their own buckets.

    What Waits Three Days?

    Every lane that lets a new party write is behind three days, public from the moment it is queued and cancellable until it runs: the vaults' selector routes and hook, registry and hub pointers; the registry's peers, router, payout hub route and new allowed vaults; the hub's selector changes and every grant through proposeLeverChange (allowing a reporter, setting the router, setting a flush or designation source, elevating a referrer). The correction lane waits three days by construction and the user may cancel at any time. Revocations never wait. The details are in Timelocks and Master Levers.

    Can Commission Be Lost?

    The First Flush Covers at Most the Last 104 Closed Weeks

    _cursorFromWeek anchors a virgin cursor at the current week minus 103, the recorder's settle bound, so closed weeks older than that never enter an envelope. A book is a measurement of present stake and the permissionless advanceBook pre walks a dormant book at any time, so the bound is reached only by a referrer whose book nobody touched for two years. Our courier flushes every book regularly.

    The Spoke Advances Its Cursors Before the Hub Applies

    _commitFlush writes the referrer's cursors in the vault transaction, while the hub applies the envelope lazily from its stored commitment. An envelope the hub could never apply would leave those weeks behind the cursor. The only sender is the vault itself, whose planner builds every envelope within the hub's bounds, and an envelope that CCIP did not deliver is recovered by executing it manually on the off ramp, which our runbook covers. The commitment store makes every received envelope applicable by anyone at any later time.

    A Zero Budget Own Flush Can Advance the Through Week Cursor

    When the planner has no work budget left for the first week of a book, _planWeeks can still store the week before it as the through week, so a courier lane opens on an empty plan. Reaching a zero budget on a virgin cursor in one call was not shown to be reachable with the shipped caps, and the referrer's own window flushReferralWindow ships any week above the cursor on demand, so nothing is lost.

    The Gap Reserve Reads Only the Record at the Cursor

    _gapWorkWaiting turns the gap lane's reserve off when the record at the cursor is exhausted, even if a later gap record still waits, so the weekly crumbs may spend the whole budget in that flush and the waiting span moves one flush later. This is a delay of one flush per cycle, never a loss; the span is shipped by the next flush.

    The Book Advanced Gate Also Holds the Referrer's Own Batch

    _checkBookAdvanced keys on a batch of more than one referrer, not on who calls, so a referrer's own multi referrer flush is refused with FlushBookNotAdvanced until the books are advanced. The permissionless advanceBook clears it, and a single referrer flush settles inline. The gate exists so one call never walks more than one book's week boundaries.

    A Vault That Is Paused or Disarmed Ships Nothing

    While a vault is paused (whenNotPaused) or its recorder is disarmed, no crumb, gap slice, self run or designation leaves it, and third parties are refused for one day after the reopen (FLUSH_OUTAGE_GRACE). The weeks are not lost: the recorder zero weights the disarm span and the referrer's own flush ships everything once the vault is back. The pause and the disarm are master actions covered by our runbook.

    A Failed Registry Read Keeps a Parked Sender's Tracked Ledger High

    When the registry lookup inside a park drain fails, the sender stays parked and its tracked contribution stays above its balance until the next entrypoint drains it, so a flush in between can report departed stake as self presence for that stretch. The registry is a master wired contract whose read paths never revert, the failure is reported by CohortReferrerLookupFailed and the very next entrypoint drains the park, so the effect is bounded to the time between two touchpoints.

    A Failed Designation Read Ships No Designation Word

    _readDesignationWord returns zero on a failed or malformed registry answer and the envelope ships without a designation word, with no event. The real registry's view cannot fail that way, the referrer's designation travels with the next flush or with a direct pushPayoutAddress, and the reader's claimablesOf shows whether a bucket is claimable.

    The First Settle of a New Self Crumb Writes No Self Log Record

    _settleSelfPieceCohort stamps a self crumb at its first settle without writing a self log record for the weeks before it, so a referrer's own presence counts from its first stamp onward. A presence record starts when the stake starts being recorded, by design; there is no retroactive presence anywhere in the system.

    A Move Whose Registry Lookup Fails Loses Its Carry

    A same chain move whose destination lookup fails, or whose carried payload exceeds the mover's post mint headroom, discards the carry wholesale (CohortCarryDiscarded), and the single event shape covers the benign and the suspicious cases alike. The move itself always completes and the destination's one way clamp keeps the book consistent; attribution is the only loss, bounded by one mover's stake. The headroom ceiling is slack by a mover's untracked balance (a balance held before arming or received by plain transfer), so we seed existing holders before arming a vault.

    A Weekly Presence Above the 80 Bit Lane Clips

    Every lane saturates at about 1.2e24 token seconds with a CohortLaneSaturated event instead of reverting, because the engine runs inside the transfer hook. At six decimals that is about two trillion USD present for a full week; every live product has six decimals.

    The Planner Clips a Gap Record Above the Lane Ceiling

    _clampGapMature clamps a dormant span's mature quantity to the lane ceiling before packing it and advances the cursor past the record, so the excess above the ceiling is never priced. The ceiling is about 2e18 base units, two trillion USD at six decimals, unreachable on every live product.

    Each Priced Cell Keeps a Sub Unit Residue

    The due of a cell is one floor over the three lanes, so at most one base unit per source, referrer and week is never paid; the remainder park is declared and unused. At six decimals this is at most one millionth of a dollar per cell.

    A Queue Leave Never Re Credits the Referrer

    joinQue escrows the queued shares through an internal transfer that runs the transfer hook, so the referee's tracked stake is clamped to the balance still held and the referrer's attributed book is reduced by the queued amount at the join, newest credit first, exactly as a sale would reduce it; the presence counted for the referrer stops for that amount from that instant. leaveQue and reduceQueAmount return the shares to the referee as plain balance through the same hook, which only clamps and never credits, so the referrer's credit is not restored, and a buyer who fills the order receives escrowed shares that no referrer is credited for. This is accepted because the queue is an exit: the join is the moment of intent to sell. Re crediting at the leave instant would let a referee cycle fresh year one credit at will, by joining and leaving in one block to turn aged or untracked shares into new credit for the cost of gas, so a new deposit is the only way credit returns.

    The Local Flush Trusts the Hub Routing

    _deliverFlushLocal calls the hub's reporter surface after the vault's cursors advanced and does not verify a state change at the hub. The hub pointer on the vault is set behind a three day timelock and the deploy order wires the hub before any vault flushes.

    Can Commission Be Paid Twice?

    The Work Cap Is Checked After the Apply Loop

    _applyFlushEnvelope prices every entry and then checks MAX_FLUSH_WORK, so an envelope above the cap would price everything and revert on every apply. Only a registered source, a master wired vault, can produce an envelope, and the vault planner bounds every envelope to 160 units on a spoke and 96 on the hub chain, so the check is a second guard against a source that does not exist.

    A Reporter That Shifts Lanes Between Two Deliveries Prices the Week Twice

    A report merges the lane sum by maximum while the cursor merges each lane by maximum, so the same average with its lanes shifted between year one, year two and mature would price again on the shifted lane. Only a registered source can report, and its crumb words are written by the recorder with fixed lane geometry, so this needs a malicious trusted reporter.

    Mutual Referrals Across Two Chains Earn Until a Sync Lands

    Two users who assign each other on two different chains before the registries exchange keys hold one direction of the pair on each chain, and each earns on the other's stake there until a sync arrives. The merge rule dissolves a mutual pair by key the moment either chain sees the other's pair, broadcastSync is permissionless and cheap, and our courier broadcasts every new assignment. A hub side check was not added because a blocked pair simply moves to two fresh wallets of the same owner; two wallets of one person that refer each other are two depositors who climb the ladder with their own stake, which is the ladder's purpose, and a user can never refer itself.

    The Local Cycle Walk Cannot See a Ring Through Another Chain

    _requireNoReferralCycle walks the local referrerOf only, so a master assignment or two assignments on two chains can form a mutual pair that the local walks do not see. The merge dissolves such a pair by key the moment the syncs meet, exactly as for the deposit lane above, and the two wallet argument applies: a cycle longer than one hop is an offchain policy matter.

    One reportSelf Call Can Carry 104 Runs of 104 Weeks

    The local reporter lane caps the run count and each run's span separately, so a maximal batch far exceeds one transaction's gas and never lands. The lane is open to master allowed vaults only and the vault planner bounds its own work; the CCIP lane is capped by work.

    The Mailbox Applies Every Self Run an Entry Names

    The envelope lane loops an 8 bit run count and relies on the post loop work cap, so a hostile envelope could price many runs before reverting. Only a registered vault sends envelopes and its planner bounds the runs, so the cap is a second guard.

    A Self Run Above the Lane Ceiling Is Refused

    _applySelfRun rejects a weekly self average above the 80 bit lane divided by one week, and the planner does not clamp before sending, so such an envelope would stay unappliable. The bound is about 2e12 USD of average own stake at six decimals; every live product has six decimals.

    The Hub Rejects a Self Run Whose Last Week It Has Not Closed

    A spoke that crossed a week boundary before the hub's clock did ships a run the hub reads as future, and the apply reverts until the hub clock passes the boundary. Weeks are Unix weeks on every chain, so the skew is seconds, and a stored commitment is applied by anyone afterwards.

    A Written Bit Only Crumb Lists a Source While Delivering Nothing

    A crumb word with only the written bit set passes the nonzero test, lists the source for the referrer and freezes its chain id. Zero lanes price zero, so no money moves; the source list of a referrer is bounded by the number of registered vaults, because only they can deliver.

    The Settle Walk Cost Grows With the Referrer's Source List

    settleWeeks re settles every listed source of a referrer for every week asked, so the keeper's cost after a rate raise grows with the sources a referrer is listed at. A referrer can be listed only at registered vaults (ten at the deploy), each call is bounded to 104 weeks, and the claimable only reads low until the keeper settles, never high.

    Anyone Can Walk Future Weeks Through settleWeeks

    The call has no closed week bound, so a caller may walk weeks whose cursors are all zero and pay the gas for nothing. It is permissionless and idempotent by design; a walk over empty weeks changes nothing.

    The Gap Lane Relies on settleWeeks After a Rate Raise

    The crumb lane tops up its own source's cell inline when its report raises the week's rates; the gap lane has no such arm and waits for settleWeeks. For the gap quantities the recorder produces the inline arm is not reachable, and the keeper's settle after every WeekRatesRaised covers both lanes.

    A Removed and Re Added Source Splits Its Bucket

    A settle credits the chain id read at settle time while the source's chain id is written at its first listing, so removing a source and re adding it under another chain id moves later top ups into another bucket. A source is never re keyed without a removal first, and a removal is a deliberate master act with no operational reason today.

    Can a Bot Take My Share?

    An Allowlisted Bot That Delivers a Referrer's Closed Weeks First Keeps the Courier Share

    Both flush paths, flushReferrals and flushReferralWindow, let an allowlisted bot ship a referrer's closed weeks above the referrer's stored cursors, and the hub pays the bot botShareBps of the commission of the weeks it priced first. The fee is paid by the protocol on top of the referrer's commission, never deducted from it, so the referrer receives the full commission whoever delivered the week. Which addresses may earn the fee is the allowlist policy of the vault's master, with one courier address per vault; a referrer who wants nothing shipped by the courier flushes its own book first, which advances the cursors the courier must stay above.

    A Referrer That Controls a Flush Bot Collects the Courier Share Itself

    A referrer whose address is on a vault's flush bot allowlist, or who controls an allowlisted address, flushes its own book through it and is paid the share by _payCourierFee. The allowlist is master's policy and holds one operator courier per vault, so this is a choice master would have to make, and the share is a protocol paid delivery fee that was budgeted for the delivery either way. A referrer's own flush, from the referrer's address, earns nothing.

    An Allowed Reporter Names Any Deliverer

    deliverCrumbs and deliverGap take the deliverer as a parameter, so a reporter could name any address and direct the courier share to it. Reporters are the mainnet vaults, allowed by master behind the lever timelock; their code passes the actual caller as deliverer and only when it is an allowlisted bot. The setter exists so a later mainnet product can be added, under the same multisig.

    A Registered Flush Source Authors the Designation Word

    The designation word in an envelope comes from the source vault, and the hub applies it with no owner signature. The source is a master deployed vault whose code reads the designation from the registry, where only the owner's own call writes it, and this is the system's standing trust boundary: every number the hub takes comes from a registered source.

    Can Someone Take My Claim?

    The Full Claim Reverts on a Short Pot

    claim sends the whole bucket and reverts PotDry when the hub holds less. This is the exact amount path by design; claimUpTo and claimAll pay the covered part and leave the rest claimable, and the reader shows both the bucket and the pot.

    One Claimant Takes the Shared Pot Cover

    _coveredAmount is the hub's whole balance with no per bucket reservation, so a short pot pays the first claimants and the others wait. Every unpaid amount stays claimable, totalPayable - totalClaimed tells the funder exactly what the pot should hold, and nothing is lost.

    An Empty Pot Makes claimAll Report Nothing Claimable

    With a zero balance every bucket clamps to zero and claimAll reverts NothingClaimable, the same error as empty buckets. The reader's claimablesOf and the hub's claimable distinguish the two cases before the call.

    claimAll Pays in the Caller's List Order

    The buckets are paid in the order the caller lists them against the shrinking cover, so a payout address that calls claimAll can put its own chain first. The payout address was designated by the referrer and every bucket pays to the referrer's designations, so the order moves the referrer's own money between the referrer's own buckets.

    A Zero Payout Designation Pays the Referrer's Own Address on Mainnet

    The registry accepts the zero address as a payout and the hub stores it with a valid stamp, so the bucket pays the referrer's own address on the hub chain. The referrer set that designation itself; a contract wallet on a spoke designates a nonzero mainnet address it controls, as Your Payout Address per Chain says.

    The Hub Local Setter Writes Under an Equal Stamp and Loses an Equal Second Tie

    setPayoutAddress on the hub has no newer stamp check and packs rank zero, so a spoke push of the same second outranks it for that bucket. Both writers act on the owner's own buckets; a spoke designation can only reach the hub from the owner's own address on that spoke.

    The Previous Payout Address Can Claim While a Newer Designation Is in Flight

    A piggybacked designation is read at plan time and applied when the envelope is applied, so for the CCIP delay the earlier address keeps the claim right. The earlier address was the owner's own earlier designation; pushPayoutAddress lands a designation directly.

    A Designation That Fails the Clock Check Is Skipped While the Entry Prices

    A stamp more than one hour ahead of the hub clock is skipped with FlushDesignationSkipped and the entry's weeks still price, so a spoke bucket stays unclaimable until a designation lands. No chain pair drifts by an hour, and pushPayoutAddress lands a fresh designation on demand.

    A Second Designation Inside One Second Reverts

    setPayoutAddress on a registry requires a strictly newer stamp, so a user who corrects a wrong payout address in the same second reverts with PayoutStampNotNewer and tries in the next block. The strict rule is what keeps two designations from racing under one stamp.

    No Path Cancels a Stored Flush Commitment

    _receiveFlush writes the commitment before any payload validation and nothing clears it, so a malformed envelope stays committed. The only sender is the vault whose planner keeps envelopes well formed, an unapplied commitment pays nothing, and a hub facet can be replaced through the selector timelock if a recovery is ever needed.

    One Sender in Both Source Maps Routes Pushes Into the Flush Path

    ccipReceive consults the flush map first, so a sender registered both as a flush source and a designation source would have its pushes decoded as envelopes. Flush sources are vaults and designation sources are registries, distinct addresses, the setters refuse re keying, and the wiring is scripted.

    What Can the Operator Do?

    A Master Assignment May Be Proposed on Any Chain, and the User Vetoes per Chain

    For a user without a referrer, proposeCorrectReferrer runs on any chain of the mesh, so a chain that never synced a user's existing pair reads that user as unassigned and master could propose there. This is our chosen shape for the assignment lane. The execute mints a mirrored key that wins every later merge, we converge a user's pair mesh wide (one broadcastSync) before proposing, the user may cancel for three days on that chain, and a first referral deposit or an arriving sync during the window changes the user's key and kills the proposal with NothingToCorrect.

    The User Vetoes Every Correction Without Limit

    cancelCorrectReferrer has no deadline and no count: a user can cancel every proposal for ever, and master can only propose again. This is the design of a consent gated lane. The registry is immutable against master alone and changes only with the user's consent, so a user who refuses can never be bound.

    A Matured Proposal Never Expires

    executeCorrectReferrer checks only that three days have passed, so master may execute a proposal long after it matured. The user keeps the cancel right until the execute, the proposal is bound to the user's exact key so any change of the user's state kills it, every proposal and cancellation is an event, and the lane is master only.

    A Corrected Pair Flips Every Peer Registry Without a Veto There

    After an execute, one broadcastSync carries the corrected pair to every peer, where the merge adopts it without a window. The veto was exercised on the origin chain, the chain whose rank the user's key carries, where the user has already acted; a referrer is one fact mesh wide, so a per chain veto would let one chain hold a stale pair for ever.

    A User Address That Cannot Call Keeps No Veto

    The veto is a plain call from the user's address, so a contract with fixed logic, or a contract account not deployed on the origin chain, cannot exercise it and the lane is master only for such users. Master is a multisig, every proposal is public for three days through ReferralCorrectionProposed, and everything already priced stays with the old referrer.

    The Old Referrer Has No Veto

    Only the user and master may cancel a correction; the old referrer is never a party. The referrer is the user's choice, the lane exists to let a user correct a wrong one with master's agreement, and the old referrer keeps every week already priced.

    A First Assignment During the Window Kills the Proposal

    A vault assignment or a sync adoption during the three days changes the user's key, so the execute reverts with NothingToCorrect and the dead proposal stays queued until master proposes again or cancels. The bind to the held key is what prevents a stale proposal from overriding a choice the user made in the meantime.

    A Second Proposal Overwrites the Pending One

    proposeCorrectReferrer for a user with an open proposal overwrites the target and restarts the window with no distinct event. Only master proposes, every proposal emits ReferralCorrectionProposed with its target, and the restart only lengthens the user's veto window.

    A Master Assignment Emits No ReferralAssigned

    An execute for a user without a referrer emits ReferralCorrectionExecuted with a zero old referrer and no ReferralAssigned. An indexer follows both events to see every pair; the distinction records which lane wrote it.

    A Master Assignment Marks No Flip

    The assignment branch of the execute skips the flip markers because there is no old referrer whose book could hold the user's stake. A dissolved mutual edge leaves no attributed stake under a zero referrer either: its marker was set at the dissolve and is delivered by pokeMigration.

    The Mirrored Assignment Key Beats a Referrer the User Names Later

    A master assignment carries the mirrored key, so a referrer the user names in a later deposit on another chain loses the merge. This is the intended effect: the lane assigns a referrer with the user's three day consent, and the result is one pair mesh wide. A user who disagrees cancels within the window.

    An Assignment on a High Rank Chain Never Lands on a Low Rank Peer

    An execute on a chain whose rank exceeds a peer's maxSyncRank mints a key that peer refuses. Every live chain has rank 3 or below and every registry accepts rank 3; before a fifth chain is peered, we raise maxSyncRank on every registry, which the deploy script asserts.

    A Sync Key Above maxSyncRank Reverts the Delivery on Every Retry

    ccipReceive reverts SyncKeyInvalid for a rank above maxSyncRank and the revert also unwinds the replay marker, so the delivery keeps failing until master raises the rank. No live chain mints such a key; the guard exists to refuse injected ranks that no registry holds, and raising the rank before peering a new chain is a deploy step.

    The Timelocked Allow Lane Can Track a Codeless Address

    executeAllowedVaultChange does not probe code.length before _trackVault takes one of the eight permanent tracked slots. The deploy script refuses a codeless configuration address, the lane is master only behind three days, and pokeMigration never calls a tracked entry without code, so a slip burns a slot and nothing else.

    Wiring Before finalizeSyncSetup Is Instant

    The sync peer, router, payout hub and allow lanes wait three days only after finalizeSyncSetup, so during the wiring phase master proposes and executes in one transaction. The wiring phase is a scripted deploy step run by the multisig master, the preflight checks that no proposal is pending at finalization, and after finalization every lane waits.

    Proposals on Master Lanes Never Expire

    The lever queue, the selector queue and the registry queues keep a matured proposal until it is executed or cancelled, and a deploy script that applies a lever executes whatever matching proposal is queued. Every lane is master only, every proposal is public from the moment it is queued (LeverChangeProposed carries the full call), and every lane has a cancel.

    The Elevation History Holds Eight Windows, and an Empty Window Takes a Slot

    elevateReferrer appends a window and reverts ElevationHistoryFull after MAX_ELEVATION_WINDOWS (8); endElevation accepts an until week equal to the from week, which stores a window that holds no week and still takes a slot. Both calls are master only, a window is a deliberate grant, and the capacity exists so pricing a week costs a bounded scan. The history is part of the hub from its first deployment, so no earlier window exists outside it.

    The Grace Freeze Switch Ships Off

    CohortHookFacet keeps the live transfer hook's grace freeze check and honours graceFreezeEnabled, which stays false at the deploy. The freeze is an existing vault policy that master can switch on at any time; shipping it off is the standing configuration, not a gap in the hook.

    The Deployment Scripts Are Operator Tooling

    The deploy scripts are run by master. A peer wiring script re run after finalization queues a proposal and resets its clock instead of converging; an abort of the flush upgrade round can leave the transfer hook proposal queued and emits cancellation events for selectors already executed; the flush hub propose phase keeps any pending proposal without comparing its target. Each of these is visible in the events and in the read only preflight that our runbook runs before every step, and every queued proposal is cancellable, so they are procedure items for the operator rather than protocol risks.

    What About the Year, the Thresholds and the Reads?

    Pricing Divides Yearly Rates by a 364 Day Year

    ReferralHubIntake._dueOf divides each week's commission by BPS_DENOMINATOR times CohortWeeks.YEAR, and CohortWeeks.YEAR is 52 weeks, 364 days, so a rate read against a calendar year of 365.25 days pays about 0.34 percent more than the stated figure (365.25 divided by 364). This is accepted because the 52 week year is the documented rate unit of the whole system. Every quantity is counted in whole weeks keyed by the Unix timestamp divided by one week: crumbs, cursors, flush envelopes, rate tables and elevation windows. The ring that ages credit from year one into year two and then mature has CohortWeeks.RING_SLOTS (52) slots, one per week, so a cohort's year one is exactly 52 weeks and ends on a week boundary; a calendar year would end mid week and one week would need two rates at once. The hub pays each week as amount times rate times seven days over 364 days, so a referrer on the 10 percent tier earns 10 percent of its weekly averages over 52 weeks. The ring and the denominator agree, so no week is paid twice or skipped, and the 0.34 percent is the whole effect. The referrer chapter explains the same thing for readers in The 52 Week Year.

    The Genesis Ladder Thresholds Are Six Decimal Units

    The thresholds compare against native unit week averages, so an 18 decimal product would top the ladder at one millionth of a token. Every live product has six decimals, a future non USD or differently scaled vault converts before it reports, and we can publish a table for future weeks at any time.

    The Rate Table Search Assumes One Table

    _lastAtOrBefore sets its upper bound to the array length minus one, so an empty table array would panic. The constructor seeds the genesis table and tables are only ever appended, so the array is never empty.

    No Getter Exposes the Elevation History

    elevationWindowsOf is internal and the hub exposes only elevatedFromWeek and elevatedUntilWeek, the mirror of the latest window. Replaying ReferrerElevated and ReferrerElevationEnded per referrer reconstructs every window, and the effective rates of any week are read exactly from weeklyRates.

    The Reader's Elevated Flag Is an Inference for Earlier Windows

    ladderStandingOf reports elevated from the latest window and otherwise infers it from an effective rate at the cap while the natural tiers do not reach it, so a week whose ladder reaches the cap on its own reads as not elevated, and a zero cap table disables the inference. The flag is display only; the rates the reader returns are the hub's own, and payments never read the flag.

    Recorder Getters Answer Zeros on a Direct Facet Call

    The CohortRecorderFacet views carry no delegatecall guard, so a call to the facet address itself reads the facet's own empty storage. Every integrator calls the vault diamond, which routes the selector to the facet under the diamond's storage; the addresses to call are the vault addresses in Addresses.

    Conclusion

    Every accepted finding falls into one of four classes: a design choice the guide documents elsewhere, an action only the multisig master or a master wired vault could take, an effect bounded to dust or to a delay, or a step our runbook covers. None of them lets a third party take money that is not theirs, lower a closed week's verdict or move a user's stake without consent.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Short answers, each with a link to the chapter that explains it in full.

    If you only have a minute, this chapter answers the questions depositors, referrers, integrators and reviewers ask most often. Every answer is two to four sentences and points to the place where the full explanation lives.

    Depositor Questions

    How Do I Name a Referrer, and Can I Change It?

    You name a referrer by depositing through a referral deposit entrypoint such as depositWithReferral, with the referrer address in the deposit; the first referrer you name is written once and shared by every vault of the chain. You cannot change it yourself afterwards. The operator can propose a different referrer, which you can veto for three days with cancelCorrectReferrer, so a change needs your consent. See Naming a Referrer and Can My Referrer Be Changed?.

    What if I Deposit Without a Referrer?

    Your deposit works exactly as before and nobody earns on it; your stake is tracked but attributed to no referrer. Because no referrer was written, a later deposit through a referral entrypoint can still name one, and from then on it earns on the stake you keep in the vaults. See For Depositors.

    Does the Referral Cost Me Anything?

    No. Your deposit, your interest and your exits are the plain vault flows, and the commission your referrer earns, like the courier share a flush bot earns, is paid from the protocol's own pot on mainnet. Nothing is deducted from your principal or your interest. See Does a Referral Cost Me Anything?.

    When Does My Referrer Earn, and When Can They Claim?

    Your referrer earns from the second your deposit lands, measured in token seconds, at the rate of each week. A week's commission becomes claimable at the hub on mainnet once the week has closed, the vault has flushed its record and the hub has applied it. See Your First Week: a Worked Example.

    What Happens if I Join the Exit Queue or Withdraw?

    Joining the queue is a withdrawal for commission purposes: the queued shares leave your referrer's book at the join, newest credit first, and leaving the queue unfilled restores nothing for the referrer. Your own position is unaffected, and a new deposit builds credit again. See What if I Join the Exit Queue?.

    Referrer Questions

    What Are the Tiers and the Cap?

    Your referred average and your own average are each placed on an eight rung ladder from 5,000 USD (1 percent) to 1,000,000 USD (8 percent), and the two tiers add up to your year one rate, capped at 10 percent. Year two credit pays 5 percent and mature credit 1 percent, only in weeks where your uncapped sum reaches the cap. See What a Referrer Earns.

    What Is an Elevated Referrer?

    An elevated referrer is priced as at the cap for every week inside an elevation window the operator granted, whatever its inputs: 10 percent on year one credit, 5 percent on year two and 1 percent on mature credit. A grant waits three days and starts at a future week; it never reaches back. See Elevated Referrers and, for integrators, The Elevated Referrer Address Rule.

    Why 52 Weeks and 364 Days?

    Everything in the system is counted in whole weeks, and the ring that ages credit has 52 weekly slots, so a cohort's year one is exactly 52 weeks and ends on a week boundary. Every rate is therefore a rate per 52 week year of 364 days, which reads about 0.34 percent higher against a calendar year, and that is the whole effect. See The 52 Week Year.

    What Is a Flush, and Who Pays for It?

    A flush is a transaction on a vault that ships a referrer's closed weeks to the hub, over CCIP from Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood and by direct call on mainnet. The caller pays the CCIP fee in the chain's native token, with any excess refunded; you may always flush your own book, and the operator's allowlisted bots flush everyone's. See Shipping Weeks to the Hub.

    What Is the Courier Share?

    When an allowlisted bot delivers closed weeks that were not yet priced, the protocol pays the bot 5 percent of the commission those weeks earned, on top of your commission and never deducted from it. Your own flush earns no share, and no other address earns one either. See Shipping Weeks to the Hub.

    Why Does a Claim Sometimes Pay Less Than Claimable?

    The pot is funded by the protocol and can be short between top ups. claim then reverts, while claimUpTo and claimAll pay what is covered in the order you list the buckets and leave the rest claimable, so nothing is lost. See What if the Pot Is Short?.

    Where Is My Commission Paid?

    At the hub on Ethereum mainnet, in USDC, into one bucket per source chain and referrer. A mainnet bucket pays your own address by default; a Base, Arbitrum or Robinhood bucket pays only after you designate a payout address on that chain's registry and it reaches the hub, and a contract wallet must designate a nonzero mainnet address it controls. See Your Payout Address per Chain.

    What Is the Sovren Vault, and Does It Count for My Ladder?

    The Sovren vault (wsnusdc on mainnet, a USDC vault whose deposits finance Sovren infrastructure nodes) is vault system 1 of the same referral umbrella; the nine World Mobile vaults are system 0. Both run under one rate table, cap, ladder, courier share and payout pot, and your ladder inputs sum over every vault of every system, so 500,000 referred in each counts as 1,000,000. Money never moves between systems, and the Sovren vault joins the referral system alongside the World Mobile rollout. See Several Vault Systems Under One Referral Umbrella and The Sovren Vault.

    What Happens to My Commission if a Bot Flushes My Weeks First?

    Nothing changes for you. The bot earns the courier share on top of your commission, and you are credited the full commission of every week it delivered. If you prefer to ship first, flush your own book, which advances the cursors a courier must stay above. See Who Controls What Is Shipped?.

    What Do I Have to Do Once?

    One own flush on every vault where your referees hold stake, so that our couriers can carry your book there from then on, and on Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood one payout designation per chain, so that the bucket of that chain can pay you; mainnet needs no designation. The flush and who may send it are in Shipping Weeks to the Hub, the designation in Your Payout Address per Chain, and the states a front end derives from them in Which Views for Which Screen.

    Integrator Questions

    How Am I Identified as a Referrer?

    By the referrer address you write into the referrer field of every deposit you send, and by nothing else. Use one address on every chain and in every deposit, and tell us that address before your first deposit if you are to be elevated. See The Elevated Referrer Address Rule.

    Which Functions Do My Users Call?

    The plain and referral deposit variants, the interest claim and compound calls, the exit queue, the moves and bridges, and on the hub the three claim calls; each is listed with its signature, caller and effect in Functions Reference. The views and events a front end uses are tabled in UI Integration.

    What Does the Referral Upgrade Change on the Vaults?

    It adds timelocked selector routes for the new facets, swaps the transfer hook through its own three day timelock and points each vault at its registry and the hub through two new pointer trios. It revokes no admin function, and mintSupply and burnSupply stay master powers unless the one way disAllowSupplyChangeByOwner is called. See Admin Functions and What the Referral Upgrade Changes.

    What if Two People Refer Each Other Across Chains?

    Two accounts cannot refer each other on one chain, and when it happens across two chains before the registries have talked, the earlier key dissolves the pair the moment a sync lands; anyone can send that sync and our courier does. The two can earn on each other only until then. See Mutual Referrals Across Two Chains Earn Until a Sync Lands.

    Safety Questions

    What Can the Operator Change, and With What Notice?

    We can publish a new rate table for future weeks only, grant reporters, sources, routers and elevations through a lever that waits three days and is public from the moment it is queued, route new facets through a three day selector timelock, and propose a referrer correction that you can veto for three days. We can never write a score, a cursor, a week bucket or a claimable, and nothing already credited is ever clawed back. See The Trust Model.

    Can a Closed Week Change?

    Its rate can only rise, when a later record for the same week raises the inputs, and the hub tops up what it already paid. No commission already credited is ever clawed back, not even after a correction of the referrer. See Can a Priced Week Change?.

    Can Someone Take Money That Is Not Theirs?

    No accepted finding lets anyone take another party's money; where a finding costs anything, it costs time, dust or an operator's gas. The full list, grouped by the question you would ask, is in Safety and Known Findings.

    Where Do I Find the Addresses?

    In the vault repository's configuration records, never in prose: the vault diamonds, USD tokens, CCIP routers, the four registries, the hub and the reader are live and verified, and the vault facet rows are filled once the selector rounds execute on the vaults. Read an address from its file and check it with cast code before you use it. See Addresses.
